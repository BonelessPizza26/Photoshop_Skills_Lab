What is a pixel?

A pixel is one tiny square of colour. Photos are made of millions of them. Zoom right in on any photo and you will see the squares.

Raster vs Vector

Photoshop is mainly raster (pixels) – it goes blurry if you enlarge it too much. Vector (Illustrator) uses maths and stays sharp at any size.

RGB vs CMYK

RGB (Red, Green, Blue) is for screens. CMYK (Cyan, Magenta, Yellow, Black) is for printing. Choose this when you make a new document.

PSD, JPG or PNG?

PSD keeps your layers (save your working file as this!). JPG is small, for photos. PNG keeps transparent backgrounds.

Resolution (DPI/PPI)

72 ppi is fine for screens and web. 300 ppi is the standard for good-quality printing.

Made a mistake?

Press Ctrl+Z (Mac: ⌘+Z) to undo. Press it again to keep going back. The History panel shows every step.
