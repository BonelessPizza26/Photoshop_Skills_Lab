🔄 Rotate, flip & Free Transform

Rotate the whole image: Image › Image Rotation › 90° Clockwise, 180°, Flip Canvas Horizontal…

Transform one layer: select the layer and press Ctrl+T (Free Transform). A box with handles appears.

  • Drag a corner to resize (it keeps the proportions).
  • Move the mouse just outside a corner to rotate (the cursor bends ↻).
  • Right-click inside the box for Flip, Skew, Distort, Perspective and Warp.
  • Press Enter to apply, Esc to cancel.
⚠️ Watch outMaking a photo bigger than it really is makes it blurry and “pixelated”. Shrinking is fine.
Convert a layer to a Smart Object first (right-click layer › Convert to Smart Object). You can then scale it down and back up again without losing quality, because Photoshop remembers the original pixels.

⛶ Cropping & composition

Cropping removes the edges of a photo. It can make a picture much stronger by cutting out distractions.

Try it: drag the box on the image to move it. Pick a ratio, then press Crop!

📐 Rule of thirdsThe grid splits the image into 9 boxes. Put important things (the sun, the boat, a horizon) on the lines or where they cross – it looks more natural than putting everything in the middle.
In Photoshop, the Crop tool’s Options Bar has other overlays: Golden Ratio, Diagonal and Triangle. Use the Perspective Crop tool to straighten photos of posters or screens taken at an angle.

🪄 Photo manipulation

Manipulation means changing a photo so it shows something that wasn’t really there. Open each section for the steps.

✂️ Cutting out an object (removing a background)
  1. Open your photo and duplicate the layer (Ctrl+J).
  2. Use Select › Subject, or the Quick Selection / Object Selection tool.
  3. Fix bits it missed: hold Shift to add, Alt to remove.
  4. Click the Add Layer Mask button at the bottom of the Layers panel. The background disappears!
  5. Hide the original layer (click its 👁️).
Select › Select and Mask has a Refine Edge brush – paint over hair and fur for a much cleaner cut-out. Set Output to “New Layer with Layer Mask”.
🧩 Compositing (combining images)
  1. Cut out the object you want (see above).
  2. Drag it with the Move tool onto the tab of your other image.
  3. Resize it with Ctrl+T so it fits the scene.
  4. Match the colour and light with a Curves or Colour Balance adjustment clipped to it (Alt+click between the layers).
  5. Add a soft black shadow on a new layer underneath it.
💡 Make it believableCheck: does the light come from the same direction? Is the size realistic? Does it have a shadow?
🧽 Removing unwanted things

Small marks: Spot Healing Brush (J). Repeating patterns or edges: Clone Stamp (S). Bigger objects: select around them with the Lasso, then Edit › Content-Aware Fill.

Generative Fill (in newer versions) uses AI to fill a selection from a text prompt. If you use it in coursework, record it in your log – it is not your own photography or drawing.
🌀 Warp & Liquify

Edit › Transform › Warp lets you bend a layer with a grid – great for wrapping a logo around a mug.

Filter › Liquify lets you push pixels like wet paint – the Forward Warp tool pushes, Bloat makes things bigger, Pucker makes them smaller.

⚠️ EthicsChanging people’s faces and bodies can be harmful and misleading. Talk about this in your evaluation if you edit people.
🎭 Double exposure effect
  1. Put a portrait on the bottom layer and a landscape on the top layer.
  2. Change the top layer’s blend mode to Screen or Lighten.
  3. Add a layer mask to the landscape and paint black with a soft brush to hide parts of it.
